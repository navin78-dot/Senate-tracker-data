Race Context

The Buckeye State is in many ways a microcosm of the country as a whole. It contains everything: a slice of Appalachia in the southeast, farmland in the northwest, decaying industrial centers, and growing cities. For the past few decades, it was (mostly) a few points more Republican than the rest of the country, including in 2008, when Barack Obama carried it by a margin three points smaller than his national margin. Since 2016, that gap has widened considerably. Donald Trump carried the state by eight points in both 2016 and 2020 and by 11 points in 2024 (when he won nationally by fewer than two points). Ohio Republicans have been conservative, but they have generally been establishment-oriented politicians who have at times broken with conservative orthodoxy, like Mike DeWine and George Voinovich.

DeWine was defeated handily by former Rep. Sherrod Brown in 2006. Brown cut a very liberal profile in Washington, yet his approval ratings held up well enough for him to win two more terms in this increasingly red state (along with some luck running in successive elections with a Democratic tilt). That streak ended in 2024, when he lost by nearly four points to Bernie Moreno, a relatively weak candidate. Now Brown is back, running in the special election for the seat JD Vance vacated to become vice president. His opponent is Sen. Jon Husted, whom DeWine appointed to the seat. Husted, a former state House speaker, secretary of state, and lieutenant governor, comes straight out of the Voinovich/DeWine wing of the party.

Brown is a skilled politician whose House district was spared in redistricting after the 2000 census because Gov. Bob Taft feared Brown would run against him. He is also running in a much better environment than he had in 2024. Polls show Brown with a lead, but he is sitting around 48 percent. For someone who has been on Ohio ballots since 1974 and is at least a familiar face to virtually every voter in the state, that isn’t a great sign. Undecided voters in a state that voted for Trump by 11 points probably lean Republican. For now, this race is a true tossup.
