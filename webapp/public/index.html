What you're looking at

Eleven Senate seats are in play this November. Democrats already hold 43 of the other 89, so they need to win 8 of these 11 to reach 51. A 50-50 Senate stays Republican because Vice President Vance breaks ties. This board takes the RealClearPolitics polling average in each race, trims it for the way polls have missed in recent elections, and turns the result into one number: the chance Democrats end up with the Senate. Everywhere on the site, blue numbers favor Democrats and red numbers favor Republicans.

First, choose how much to trust the polls

In the last two midterms (2018 and 2022), Senate polls made Democrats look a little stronger than they turned out to be, especially in states that lean Republican. So before this board counts a Democratic lead, it shaves a bit off it first. There are two ways of deciding how much to shave off, and you pick one. Every number on the site is then shown under your choice, and you can switch at any time.

Tap one of the two to open the board. No numbers are shown until you choose, and you can change your mind at any time.

How to read one row

The headline number is not a seat count. It comes from running the election many times with the polls allowed to be wrong, partly in the same direction everywhere (a national miss) and partly race by race, and counting how often Democrats reach 51. Close seats can go either way, so the chance can sit well below 50% even when Democrats lead in eight races.

How the headline number has moved

The shaded halves show which side is favored: above 50% Democrats, below 50% Republicans. The solid line is the board under the rules you picked at the top of the page; the thin dashed line is the other set of rules. The market line is Polymarket. "Raw polls" is what the chance would be with no fade at all. Before October 24 the history comes from Navin's twice-daily board; since then the site reads the relay itself about every half hour.

Call it: the rules

One call per person. Pick a winner in each of the 11 races and say by how many points. Saving again replaces your earlier call; there is no history, only your current call.
Change your mind as often as you like until the deadline. After that every call is locked and nothing can be changed.
Scoring, race by race as each one is called. One point for every race where you picked the winner. Ties are broken by your margins: for each called race we take the gap between the margin you predicted and the real one (a pick of Talarico by 2 against a result of Paxton by 3 is 5 points off), add those gaps up, and the smaller total wins the tie.
After Election Day the results fill in on their own. Nothing is typed into the site: as races are called, the results are scanned in, the greyed-out results card below comes alive, and the leaderboard replaces the plain list.
